LESCO Peak Hours — Timings and How They Affect Your Bill

Peak hours are the 4 hours every evening when electricity demand is highest and the price per unit is higher — but only if you have a time-of-use (TOU) meter. Most homes in Lahore, Okara, Sheikhupura, Nankana Sahib and Kasur are on the flat domestic tariff, where a unit costs the same at 8 in the morning and 8 in the evening. For them, peak hours do not change the bill at all.

LESCO peak hours — timings by season

The peak window is always exactly 4 hours long. Only its position changes through the year, following sunset and the evening demand spike. Timings below are from the NEPRA tariff determination for LESCO, 7 January 2026 — the same four windows apply across all ex-WAPDA distribution companies, seven days a week.

SpringMarch – May6:00 PM – 10:00 PMRemaining 20 hours

Right now (November 2026), the peak window is 6:00 PM – 10:00 PM. From 1 December it moves one hour earlier, to 5:00 PM – 9:00 PM, because the sun sets earlier and evening demand starts sooner.

Does the peak rate apply to you?

The peak rate applies only where a TOU meter is installed and the connection is billed on a TOU tariff. In practice that mainly means:

  • Homes with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more — billed under tariff A-1(b) with a TOU meter.
  • Shops, offices and industries with 5 kW or more — billed on TOU tariffs such as A-2(c) once a TOU meter is installed.
  • Your tariff code — a TOU connection usually has a tariff code ending in “T” (for example A-1(T)).

If your connection is a normal home connection below 5 kW on the flat A-1 domestic tariff, the peak window does not change what you pay. Your bill charges every unit at the same slab rate, whatever the time of day.

How to check peak hours on your own bill

  1. Look at the tariff code on your bill. A code ending in “T” means a TOU tariff.
  2. Look for separate peak and off-peak unit lines. A TOU bill shows your units split into peak and off-peak. If your bill has no such split — only one units figure — you are on the flat tariff and peak hours do not apply to you.
  3. Compare the two lines. On a TOU bill, the peak-hours units are the expensive ones. Shifting heavy use out of the 4-hour window is what lowers the next bill.

How to save during peak hours

If you are on a TOU tariff, the saving comes from when you use power, not just how much:

  • Move heavy loads out of the 4 hours — washing machine, water pump, iron and geyser are the big ones. Run them before the window starts or after it ends.
  • Pre-cool the room — run the AC before peak hours, then keep it on low during the window instead of starting it at full load in the evening.
  • Charge and heat early — charge batteries, heat water and finish cooking-related heavy use before the window.
  • Check your bill every month — the peak/off-peak split on the bill shows whether the shifting is actually working. Tafseel: Understanding Your LESCO Bill.

No rupee rates on this page — on purpose. Published peak and off-peak rates differ between sources, so this page does not quote a rate. Your own bill's peak and off-peak lines are the only reliable figures for your connection. If a website quotes you a “peak rate” without showing its source, treat it as unverified.

Frequently asked questions — LESCO Peak Hours

What are LESCO peak hours right now?

In November 2026 the peak window is 6:00 PM – 10:00 PM. From 1 December 2026 it moves to 5:00 PM – 9:00 PM for the winter months (December – February).

Do peak hours affect every LESCO consumer?

No. Peak hours change the bill only for consumers with a time-of-use (TOU) meter — mainly connections with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more. Most homes on the flat A-1 domestic tariff pay the same rate all day.

How do I know if I have a TOU meter?

Check your bill: a tariff code ending in “T”, or separate peak and off-peak unit lines, means a TOU connection. If your bill shows only one units figure, you are on the flat tariff.

Can I change my peak hours or move to off-peak only?

No — the peak window is fixed by the NEPRA tariff, not by the consumer. What you can change is when you use heavy appliances: run them outside the 4-hour window.

My bill has no peak-hours line — is something wrong?

No. A bill with no peak/off-peak split simply means you are on the flat domestic tariff, where peak hours do not apply. Nothing is missing from your bill.
