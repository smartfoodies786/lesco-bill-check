LESCO Previous Bill — How to See Your Old Bills

Honest answer first: the official portal shows your current bill. There is no verified way to browse old bills month by month on the portal — this page does not claim any 12-month history feature, because none is verified. What you can do is keep your own record, and use the next bill to check the last one.

Your own record — save each month's bill as a PDF

The reliable method is simple: every month, when your new bill appears on the portal, download it and save it before the next bill replaces it.

  1. Open your current bill on the official portal: How to Check LESCO Bill Online.
  2. Download or print it as a PDF and save it in a folder on your phone or computer — one file per month, named by month (for example “LESCO Nov 2026”).
  3. Keep the paper bills too, if you still receive them. A printed or PDF copy from the portal is a duplicate bill — it shows the same reference number, amount and due date as the original, and it is accepted for payment at a bank counter or post office just like the original.

A small folder of monthly PDFs costs nothing and saves you a trip to the office later. Set a monthly reminder so you never miss a month: LESCO Bill Reminder.

What the next bill tells you about the last one

Even without the old bill in hand, the new bill tells you the important part:

  • Arrears line — if any amount from a previous bill was not paid, it appears as arrears on the next bill, and the total payable includes it. If there are no arrears, the previous bill was paid in full.
  • Late Payment Surcharge (LPS) line — if a previous bill was paid after its due date, a 10% surcharge appears as its own line. That tells you the old bill was paid late, not that it is still unpaid.
  • Reading comparison — comparing the units line with last month's bill is how you tell whether a high bill is a reading jump, a slab change, or an adjustment. Tafseel: Understanding Your LESCO Bill.

Lost all copies? Get a duplicate from the office

If you have no saved copy of an old bill, the LESCO office can help. Visit your Customer Service Centre (CSC) or SDO office with your original CNIC and a document that proves the property or connection is yours. The office staff can look up your connection and give you a duplicate bill — free, the same day. Take your reference number with you if you have it; if you have lost it too, see LESCO Bill Without Reference Number.

Why keep old bills at all?

  • Reading disputes — an old bill is the only proof of what the meter reading was last month. See Wrong Bill Complaint.
  • Proof of payment — together with your payment receipts, old bills prove a month was paid. See How to Check If Your LESCO Bill Is Paid.
  • Name change and tariff questions — the office asks for old bills when you apply for a name change or a tariff correction.

Start this month. The portal only ever shows the current bill, so the record you build yourself is the only record you control. Download this month's bill today — next month's bill will replace it on the portal.

Frequently asked questions — LESCO Previous Bill

Can I see last year's bills on the LESCO portal?

No verified way exists. The portal shows your current bill. To see old bills, use the PDFs you saved each month, or get a duplicate from the LESCO office with your CNIC.

Is a duplicate bill the same as the original?

Yes. A duplicate bill printed or saved as PDF from the official portal shows the same reference number, amount and due date as the original, and it is accepted at banks and post offices just like the original.

How long should I keep old bills?

Keep payment receipts for 6 months. Keep the bills themselves as long as you may need them for a dispute, a complaint or a name change — a small folder of PDFs costs nothing.

I need an old bill for a complaint — what should I do?

Use your saved PDF if you have one. If not, visit your CSC or SDO office with your CNIC and a property document — the office can give you a duplicate bill free the same day.

Does the arrears line mean my old bill was unpaid?

Yes. Arrears are unpaid amounts from earlier months, added to the new bill's total. If the arrears line is empty, the previous bill was paid in full.
