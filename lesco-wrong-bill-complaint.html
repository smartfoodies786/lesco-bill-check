LESCO Wrong Bill / Overbilling Complaint

If your LESCO bill is much higher than usual, do not just pay it and hope next month is better. First work out why it is high — the cause decides the fix. A reading error, an estimated bill, a wrong tariff, a faulty meter and a detection bill are five different problems with five different remedies.

First: is it really wrong? Check before you complain

Before filing a complaint, compare this bill with last month's, line by line: units used, slab and protected status, FPA and QTA, arrears and LPS. A bill can be high for a fair reason — more units used, a hot month, losing protected status, or a bigger FPA/QTA. If one of those explains the amount, it is not a wrong bill. See Understanding Your LESCO Bill and LESCO Protected vs Unprotected Consumers. If none of them explains it, work through the five causes below.

The 5 causes of a wrong bill

1. Reading error — the meter was read wrongly

Signs: the current reading on the bill is far above (or below) what your meter actually shows, or the units used are far higher than any previous month with no change in your usage.

What to do: take a dated photo of your meter showing the current reading, and compare it with the reading printed on the bill. If the bill's reading is higher than the meter's, the bill is wrong. This is the most common overbilling cause, and the dated photo is the key evidence.

2. Estimated bill — no actual reading was taken

Signs: the bill is based on an estimate rather than a real meter reading — the units look like an average or a guess, and the reading on the bill does not match your meter.

What to do: take a dated photo of your meter and file a complaint asking for the bill to be corrected to the actual reading. Keep taking dated meter photos each month so an estimated bill can be challenged with proof.

3. Wrong tariff — you are billed in the wrong category

Signs: the tariff category on the bill does not match your connection — for example, a domestic home billed as commercial, or a protected consumer billed at unprotected rates. The units may be right, but the per-unit rate is wrong.

What to do: check the tariff/category printed on your bill against your connection type, and check your protected status against the 6-month, 200-unit rule. Ask LESCO to correct the tariff category and re-issue the bill at the right rate.

4. Faulty meter — the meter itself is misreading

Signs: the meter runs even when everything in the house is switched off, the reading jumps far more than your usage can explain month after month, or the meter display is damaged or blank.

What to do: take dated photos or a short video of the meter, note the readings over a few days, and ask LESCO to test the meter. Do not open or tamper with the meter yourself — that can lead to a detection bill (see below).

5. Detection bill — charged for suspected theft or tampering

Signs: a very large extra charge appears on the bill, described as a detection bill, usually after LESCO staff found suspected meter tampering or unauthorised use.

What to do: a detection bill is a formal charge, not a reading mistake — it needs a formal complaint, not just a phone call. Gather your bills, meter photos and any LESCO notices, and complain through CCMS+ (below) and at your sub-division office. If you did not tamper with the meter, say so clearly in the complaint and ask for the evidence behind the detection charge.

Evidence to gather before you complain: a dated photo of your meter showing the current reading (take it the same day you complain); the current bill and the previous 2–3 bills, for comparison; payment receipts for past bills (keep them 6 months); and any LESCO notice or letter, if you received one. A complaint with a dated meter photo is far stronger than one without.

How to complain — CCMS+

LESCO complaints go through CCMS+, the online complaint system:

  • Online: ccms.pitc.com.pk/complaint
  • Email: ccms@pitc.com.pk
  • Helpline: 118 — for urgent issues, and to follow up a complaint already filed.

When you file the complaint, give: your 14-digit reference number, the billing month, what is wrong (reading error, estimated bill, wrong tariff, faulty meter or detection bill), the reading on the bill versus the reading on your meter, and attach the dated meter photo. Keep the complaint/reference number you are given, and keep a copy of everything you submit.

Correction timeline — 1 to 2 billing cycles

Once a complaint is accepted, correction typically takes 1–2 billing cycles. That means the corrected amount usually appears on the next bill, or the one after — not instantly. While the complaint is being processed:

  • Do not ignore the bill entirely: ask LESCO (helpline 118 or your sub-division office) whether you should pay the undisputed part, or the average of recent bills, while the complaint is open — paying nothing can add arrears and a late payment surcharge.
  • Keep the complaint reference number and check the next bill to confirm the correction has been applied.
  • If the correction does not appear after 2 billing cycles, follow up on the same complaint reference number — through CCMS+, by email, or by calling 118 — rather than starting a new complaint.

Tip: start the habit now — a dated meter photo every month, on or just after the reading date, takes a minute and is the single best protection against a wrong bill you cannot prove.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay a wrong bill while I complain?

Ask LESCO first (helpline 118 or your sub-division office) whether to pay the undisputed part or an average amount while the complaint is open. Paying nothing can add arrears and a 10% late payment surcharge.

How long does a LESCO bill correction take?

Typically 1–2 billing cycles after the complaint is accepted. Check the next two bills to confirm the correction.

What is the best evidence for a wrong-bill complaint?

A dated photo of your meter showing the actual reading, plus the current bill and the previous 2–3 bills for comparison.

Where do I file a LESCO complaint online?

Through CCMS+ at ccms.pitc.com.pk/complaint, or by email to ccms@pitc.com.pk. The helpline is 118.
