Understanding Your LESCO Bill

Most LESCO bills look confusing because the total is made of several different charges added together. Read the bill line by line, in this order, and you can usually see exactly why the amount is what it is.

This is how a LESCO bill is built up, top to bottom. Every line is explained in detail below.

1. Meter readings and units used

At the top of the bill are the previous reading and the current reading from your meter. The difference between them is the number of units (kWh) you used this month.

  • Check that the current reading is higher than the previous reading, and that the current reading is close to what your meter actually shows today.
  • If the reading looks far too high, or the bill says "estimated" instead of showing a real reading, that is the first thing to question — see LESCO Wrong Bill / Overbilling Complaint.
  • Take a dated photo of your meter each month, on or just after the reading date, so you can compare it with the bill.

2. Slabs — the price per unit depends on how much you used

Electricity is charged in slabs: the more units you use in a month, the higher the per-unit rate for the higher slabs. So a month with more units costs more per unit on the extra units, not just more in total. Which slab you fall in — and whether you are a protected or unprotected consumer — makes a large difference to the bill. See LESCO Protected vs Unprotected Consumers.

The exact per-unit rates for each slab change when NEPRA issues a new notification. This guide does not publish slab rates, because a rate without its notification date goes out of date quickly — check the rates printed on your current bill, and the current NEPRA notification via lesco.gov.pk.

3. FPA — Fuel Price Adjustment

FPA is an adjustment added to (or sometimes subtracted from) your bill to reflect changes in the cost of fuel used to generate electricity. It is charged per unit used, and it changes from month to month. A high FPA in a month when fuel costs rose can make the bill noticeably higher even if your units stayed the same. The FPA amount for the month is printed as its own line on the bill. For November 2026 bills, the FPA is Rs 2.3673 per unit (NEPRA decision of 6 November 2026, for September 2026 fuel) — see LESCO Bill Updates — November 2026 for the current figure, as it changes every month.

4. QTA — Quarterly Tariff Adjustment

QTA is another periodic adjustment, applied quarterly, to reflect changes in generation and distribution costs. Like FPA, it is shown as a separate line on the bill and varies from quarter to quarter. If your bill jumps in a quarter when a new QTA applies, that adjustment is often part of the reason. For September–November 2026 bills, the QTA is Rs 0.5194 per unit (S.R.O. 1501).

5. Taxes and other fixed charges

Your bill includes taxes and charges on top of the electricity cost. These appear as separate lines, and the amounts are set by the government and the regulator — they are not decided by LESCO. Typical lines on a LESCO bill include:

  • GST (General Sales Tax) — charged on the electricity cost.
  • Electricity duty — a duty charged on the bill.
  • PTV fee — the television licence fee shown on electricity bills.
  • Withholding tax (WHT) — applies on larger bills, above a set threshold.
  • Fixed charges — a fixed monthly charge based on your sanctioned load (kW).
  • Meter rent — a small monthly rent for the meter.
  • F.C. surcharge — a per-unit surcharge shown as its own line.

The rates and thresholds for these charges change over time. This guide does not publish specific tax rates or amounts, because the analysis behind it gives no dated, NEPRA-cited figures for LESCO — read the amounts printed on your current bill, and check the current notification if a tax line looks different from last month.

6. Arrears — unpaid amounts from earlier months

If any amount from a previous bill was not paid, it appears as arrears on the next bill, and the total payable includes it. If you paid the previous bill but it still shows as arrears, your payment may not have been recorded — find the payment receipt and contact LESCO with it. Keep receipts for 6 months for exactly this reason (see How to Pay LESCO Bill Online).

7. LPS — Late Payment Surcharge

If a previous bill was paid after its due date, a Late Payment Surcharge of 10% is added. It appears as its own line. Paying on or before the due date each month avoids this charge entirely.

Worked check — why is my bill high this month? Compare, line by line, with last month's bill: (1) units used — did they go up? (2) slab — did you cross into a higher slab? (3) protected status — did you lose protected status? (4) FPA and QTA — are the adjustment lines bigger? (5) arrears or LPS — is an old amount included? In most cases, one of these five explains the jump.

Frequently asked questions

What is FPA on my LESCO bill?

Fuel Price Adjustment — a per-unit adjustment reflecting changes in fuel costs for power generation. It changes monthly and is shown as a separate line.

What is QTA on my LESCO bill?

Quarterly Tariff Adjustment — a periodic adjustment applied each quarter, shown as a separate line on the bill.

Why did my bill go up when I used the same units as last month?

Usually because of FPA or QTA changes, a slab change, losing protected status, or arrears/LPS from a previous bill being added. Compare the lines with last month's bill to find which one moved.

Where is the due date on the bill?

The due date is printed on the bill and shown on the official portals when you check the bill online. Paying after it adds a 10% late payment surcharge.
